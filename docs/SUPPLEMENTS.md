# Supplement nutrition

The supplement card shows three nutrient values: **protein, carbohydrates and
sugars**, in grams. It shows the total for the selected local day, a consumed
product's subtotal and each recorded intake. Other nutrients remain available
in the food diary but do not crowd the supplement card.

Powder mass and dietary protein are different quantities. For a powder labelled
80 g protein, 10 g carbohydrates and 3.33 g sugars per 100 g, a 30 g intake shows
about 24 g protein, 3 g carbohydrates and 1 g sugars. Sugar is part of the
carbohydrate amount, not an extra amount added on top of it.

## Record a known product

Mark a food as a supplement when adding it to a meal, or link a supplement
product to a food with label nutrition and provide the serving weight. Recording
the product creates a linked meal snapshot. Changing the recorded amount scales
that snapshot; a later food-catalogue update does not rewrite old consumption.

The linked meal and supplement intake are one nutritional contribution. Showing
the same values in the diary and supplement card does not add them twice to daily
nutrition. Deleted records no longer contribute.

An intake without a linked food snapshot has unknown nutrition. The card shows
an em dash for an entirely unknown value, or a known subtotal plus `?` when some
contributions are unreported. It does not infer protein from the powder's weight
or assume that an unlabelled creatine product has zero carbohydrate or sugar.

## Water planning

Neither powder mass nor protein grams automatically prescribe extra litres.
Optional personal water extras are explained in [water planning](HYDRATION.md),
including the protein-metabolism evidence and its limits. Actual drinking-water
records are separate from supplement consumption.
